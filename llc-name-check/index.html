FAQ

Is your LLC name available? Quick answers.

How do I check if an LLC name is available?

Search your state's official business entity database, usually run by the Secretary of State. Our Free LLC Name Check checks your name against common state naming rules and sends you straight to the official search for your state.

Is the LLC name check free?

Yes. Checking and prepping your name is free. When you form your LLC, you pay only the state's filing fee.

What does "distinguishable" mean for a business name?

Most states require your name to be "distinguishable on the record" from existing businesses. Adding "The," punctuation, or a different ending like "Inc." vs "LLC" usually doesn't make a name different enough. Change a real word instead.

Does my LLC name have to include "LLC"?

In nearly every state, yes. The name must end with "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." Some states also allow forms like "Ltd. Liability Co." The name check confirms you've included a valid ending.

Are there words I can't use in an LLC name?

Words like Bank, Trust, Insurance, University or Attorney usually need approval from a regulator or a professional license. Words that suggest you're a corporation ("Inc.," "Corp.") or a government agency aren't allowed. The name check flags these words for you.

Can I reserve an LLC name before I file?

Most states let you reserve a name for a fee, usually for 60 to 120 days. If you're ready, filing your LLC right away also secures the name.

How much does it cost to form an LLC?

State filing fees range from about $35 (Montana) to $500 (Massachusetts). Some states add annual reports or publication costs; New York, for example, requires publication. We show your state's fee alongside your result. Always confirm it on the official state site.

Does a name check guarantee my LLC name will be approved?

The state makes the final decision when you file. Our check and the official search give you the best possible read before you file.

Is an LLC name the same as a trademark?

No. State registration stops another LLC in that state from using the same name. A trademark protects your brand in commerce, and you can search for trademarks for free at the USPTO.

Do I need a separate domain name and social handles?

It's smart to grab a matching domain and social handles once your name looks clear, so your brand stays consistent.
